# What I build on

Seed-count planning in reinforcement learning is not new. My contribution is a small **prospective calibration test**: I estimate a budget from pilot training runs, commit that budget, train fresh independent seeds, and measure how often the planned comparison detects a difference. An 80% calculation is a promise under model assumptions, not a guarantee from one noisy pilot.

- **Henderson et al. (AAAI 2018), [Deep Reinforcement Learning that Matters](https://arxiv.org/abs/1709.06560).** They document variance from seeds, hyperparameters and implementation choices, and call for uncertainty and meaningful statistical comparisons. I do not claim to introduce reproducibility or uncertainty reporting.
- **Colas, Sigaud and Oudeyer (2018), [How Many Random Seeds?](https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.08295).** They already explain statistical power, seed counts, t tests, bootstrap intervals and the consequences of assumption violations in deep RL. I use established power calculations rather than claiming a new seed-count formula. The extension here is pilot-to-fresh-seed calibration across independently repeated comparisons, with a committed confirmation plan.
- **Agarwal et al. (NeurIPS 2021), [Deep Reinforcement Learning at the Edge of the Statistical Precipice](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.13264), [rliable](https://github.com/google-research/rliable).** Their interval estimates, interquartile means and performance profiles address reliable aggregate benchmark evaluation. My per-task mean-return seed budgets answer a narrower question and do not replace their aggregate methods. Public per-run scores allow a historical, conditional power audit; they cannot establish population power from five observed runs.
- **Patterson et al. (2023 preprint; JMLR 2024), [Empirical Design in Reinforcement Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.01315v1).** Their guidance on experimental units, hyperparameter selection and experimenter bias motivates fixed comparisons, disjoint pilot/confirmation seeds and reporting every planned result. Evaluation episodes are not independent training seeds.
- **Albers and Lakens (2018), [When power analyses based on pilot data are biased](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jesp.2017.09.004).** Noisy pilot effect estimates are an established source of misplanning. I compare an observed-gap rule with a meaningful-effect rule fixed before pilot outcomes, rather than portraying this known statistical concern as a discovery.

I match the style of [eval-power](https://github.com/mottopanikeiku/eval-power): modest claims, raw data, executable calculations and separate historical resampling from new measurements. I adapt affine ARS/CEM and checked Gymnasium dynamics from [control-clock](https://github.com/mottopanikeiku/control-clock) (MIT), with original source and third-party notices retained. These short-budget control policies are deliberately cheap; the result will not establish seed requirements for deep RL generally.
